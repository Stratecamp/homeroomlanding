Here's the actual difference.

A shared calendar tells everyone what's happening and still leaves one person doing all the remembering, assigning, and reminding. That's the part we went after.

What a family calendar gives you

  • 📅Shared events everyone can see
  • 🎨A color for each person
  • 🔔Reminders, which somebody still had to enter
  • 📝A list feature you'll use twice
  • 💸A monthly bill, forever

  • 🍽️Dinner on every single day with a cook's name on it
  • 🔁Chores that rotate themselves on Sunday, so nobody nags
  • 🥜Allergy swaps written on the meal, so anyone can cook or shop
  • 📺A kitchen wall display kids check instead of hollering for you
  • 🎉A Wins Wall, because somebody should be keeping track of the good stuff
  • 🐾A column for the dog, obviously

What it actually does at 7 a.m.

Every feature here exists because something went wrong in our house first.

🍽️
Dinner is never a question

Every day has a meal and a cook attached. Claim your nights on Sunday and the empty slots do the asking, which is a much nicer job than you doing it at 4:40 in the afternoon.

📺
The kitchen wall

Any old tablet you've got in a drawer becomes the family display: today's schedule, today's chores, tonight's dinner. Check a chore off on your phone and it disappears from the wall while everyone watches. Our kids fight over who gets to do it.

🔁
Chores with streaks

Rotations flip on their own every Sunday. Keep your streak all week and you win the Friday pick. Turns out gamification works on children and, we regret to report, on adults.

🥜
Allergy notes that travel

The swap lives on the meal itself. "Dairy free: coconut milk." Now your partner can cook it and your teenager can shop for it without a single text to you.

🎉
The Wins Wall

Lost teeth, chapter books finished under the covers, second place at the dance showcase, the dog finally passing obedience. In a busy week the good stuff evaporates. This is where it lives instead.

📱
It syncs to your real calendar

Family events flow into Apple and Google calendars, so your work life and your home life finally agree on what Thursday looks like.

Why we built it instead of buying one

I build systems for enterprises for a living. Meanwhile at home I was the system: everyone's schedules, the food allergies, the laundry rotations, the vet appointments, when the grocery order locks, which kid outgrew their shoes. None of it written anywhere. All of it in my head.

We priced the wall calendar gadgets. A few hundred dollars, then a subscription on top. We tried the family apps, and they were fine until the thing we actually needed sat behind a paywall, or the interface was so grown-up that our kids never opened it once.

So we built our own, and the surprising part wasn't the software. It was what happened when the invisible work became visible. David started claiming dinners because he could see the empty Thursday. Josiah walked the dog because the rotation said so, not because I remembered to remind him. The kids stopped asking me what was happening and started checking the wall.

It's been running our house for months now, through school years and sick days and a lot of guests. People keep asking how to get it. So we're getting it ready for you.

— Endia Leonard, Founder · Phineas posts Tuesdays 🐾
